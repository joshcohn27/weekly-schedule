Quick Start

This page gets you from an empty page to a finished session. Each step is explained in full later in the manual.

  1. Open the page. The first time, a panel called How to use this page opens by itself. Read it or press Close. The ? beside the title brings it back.
  2. You are on the Setup tab. The page always opens there. It has four numbered steps, top to bottom.
  3. Step 1, Session. Pick Session 1 (4 weeks) or Session 2 (3 weeks, with Color War).
  4. Step 2, Bunks. The session starts with its bunks already in, village by village. Change names, grades and camper counts so they match this summer, and add or remove bunks. What you change here is changed in every week.
  5. Step 3, Numbers and calendar. Setup says whether the numbers add up for these bunks. To move a trip or change a number, press Look at the settings.
  6. Step 4, Build the schedule. Press Auto generate.
  7. Choose The whole session, week by week and press Generate.
  8. Wait. A bar shows how far it has got. Most runs take a few seconds, and a full camp of 27 bunks takes 2 minutes at the very most. You can keep using the page.
  9. Look at the Schedule tab for each week, and the Tracking tab to see that the counts are fair. Not happy? Press Auto generate again for a different version, or Undo.
  10. Save it. On Setup, at the bottom, press Download all weeks (.xlsx). That file is your backup, and it is how you move the schedule to another computer.
The one thing to remember: the schedule lives in this browser on this computer. Download an Excel file whenever you have something worth keeping.

1. What the app is

The Weekly Period Schedule Builder holds the camp's period schedule: every bunk, four periods a day, Sunday to Friday, for each week of a session. You can fill it in by hand, or have the app build a week or a whole session for you and then change what you like.

It is for whoever makes the schedule: a director or a program staffer. If you are comfortable with a spreadsheet you will be comfortable here.

Where your work is kept

There is no login and nothing is sent anywhere. Everything you enter is saved by your web browser, on the computer you are using. That has three consequences.

  • Another computer, or another browser on the same computer, starts empty. To move a schedule, download it as an Excel file and upload it on the other one.
  • Clearing your browser's site data erases the schedule. So does using a private or incognito window, once it is closed.
  • The Excel file is your backup. Download one before any big change, and at the end of each working session.

Session 1 and Session 2 are kept separately. Switching from one to the other does not lose either.

2. A tour of the page

The top of the page

  • ? beside the title opens How to use this page, a short guide.
  • Under the title is the session you are in, with its dates. You change session on the Setup tab.
  • The six tabs: Setup, Build, Schedule, Tracking, Specialists, Settings.

Setup

Everything that is decided once for a session, in the order you do it. Nothing on this tab is about one week: it is all for the whole session.

  1. Session. Two boxes, Session 1 and Session 2, each with its dates. The one you are in is outlined. Click the other to switch; each keeps its own bunks, schedule and settings.
  2. Bunks. A box for each village with its bunks: name, grades, campers. The count at the top right, such as "22 of 27 cabins", is how many bunks the session has out of the cabins the camp has. Chapter 3 explains the buttons.
  3. Numbers and calendar. One line saying whether the numbers add up for these bunks, and a button to the Settings tab.
  4. Build the schedule. Auto generate, or Go to the Build tab to fill periods in by hand.

Reset, in red at the top right, clears the periods or puts the session back to its base template (chapter 3). At the bottom, under Files, are Upload a schedule (.xlsx) and Download all weeks (.xlsx).

The week bar

The three tabs that show one week at a time have a bar over them.

  • Week picks the week you are looking at. Each week shows its dates.
  • Auto generate builds the periods for you.
  • Download Week N (.xlsx) saves the week on screen as an Excel file.
  • Reset is the same Reset as on Setup.

While Auto generate is working, and just after, its progress and its result show in this bar on every tab.

Build

This is where the periods of one week are entered and changed. Each row is a bunk: its name, grades and number of campers, then a box for every period of the week. The day headings carry the dates. The bunks themselves are not changed here: that is done on Setup.

Above the grid are two tools, Set a whole period and Clear, both explained in chapter 3.

Schedule

The finished grid, ready to print. Bunks doing the same thing in the same period are merged into one box. Click a box to light up every box in the same program area; a league lights up within its village. The Print button prints it.

Tracking

How many times each bunk has each program area, for the week on screen and for the whole session. This is the place to check that things are fair. The last row, All bunks, adds everything up. Pointing at a number lights up its bunk and its program area, so it is easy to read across a wide table.

Specialists

The schedule from one program area's side. Pick the area, and each week is a grid with days across and periods down. Each box says who comes, which visit it is for them in that area (counted from the start of the session), and how many campers. When a whole village comes it is written by name: Onondaga, Cayuga, Seneca, Mohawk, Tusc or Taste. Print it for the specialist.

Settings

The numbers Auto generate works with: how often each program area happens, how many bunks it takes at once, who may share a period, and the session calendar. Chapter 7 lists every setting.

3. How to do things by hand

Enter and edit bunks

Bunks are set on the Setup tab, in step 2. A bunk belongs to the session: what you do to it there is done in every week.

  • Type in a bunk's name, grades and campers boxes.
  • The first letter of the name is the village. O is Onondaga, C is Cayuga, S is Seneca, M is Mohawk, T is Tusc. A bunk named O3 is in Onondaga. If you rename a bunk so that it starts with another letter, it moves to that village's box.
  • Grades can be written the way you write them: 4th, 4th/5th, 9th. The app uses them to decide which bunks are close enough in age to share.
  • The camper count matters at the pool, at ropes, at Yoga and at Ceramics, which go by how many campers fit.
  • Names that start with TC (TC1, TC2 and so on) are Taste of CSL. See chapter 4.
  • The arrows at the end of a row move a bunk up or down the list. Remove takes it out of every week, with everything on its rows. It asks first.
  • Add a bunk, under a village, adds the next bunk of that village (after O5 comes O6) to every week, with a starting grade. Give it its camper count.

The camp has 27 cabins, so a session can have no more than 27 bunks, Taste of CSL included. At 27 the add buttons go grey.

Fill in the biggest camp brings every village up to its most bunks in every week. In Session 1 that is 5 Onondaga, 6 Cayuga, 6 Seneca, 6 Mohawk and 4 Tusc. In Session 2 it is 4 Taste of CSL, 3 Onondaga, 4 Cayuga, 6 Seneca, 6 Mohawk and 4 Tusc, because Taste of CSL's cabins come out of Onondaga's and Cayuga's. It also sets every bunk to 15 campers. The 15 is only a round number to plan with; nothing holds a bunk to it. It asks first.

On the Build tab the bunk's name, grades and campers are shown and cannot be typed in. That is on purpose: a bunk is the same in every week, so it is changed in one place.

Fill a period by hand

Click a period's box and pick an activity from the list, or type to search. Activities are grouped by program area.

  • Write-ins. Type anything that is not in the list and it is kept as written. A write-in is not counted on the Tracking tab.
  • Hobbies fill everyone. Picking AM Hobbies or PM Hobbies in one bunk's box fills that half-day for every bunk.
  • A league fills its village. Picking League, CHL, SSL, MNL or MAL fills that period for the whole village.
  • Some activities are always double periods: Low Ropes, High Ropes, Waterfront, MNL and MAL fill both periods of the half-day you clicked in.
  • Changing one box afterwards only changes that one box.

Set a whole period

Choose the day, the period (or all day, morning, afternoon), All bunks or one village, and the activity. Then press the button, which reads Set for all bunks or Set for Onondaga. This is the quick way to enter a trip.

Enter a trip

Trips, Tiyuls and bike trips are on the session calendar and go onto the schedule by themselves (chapter 4). To put one somewhere else by hand, use Set a whole period with the activity Tiyul, Bike Trip or Trip for the village and the periods it is away. A trip you enter by hand stays where you put it, and the calendar does not add a second one for that village.

Clear an area, a bunk or a village

The Clear tool empties part of the week in one go: everything or one program area, for all bunks, one village or one bunk, on one day or the whole week, all day or one part of it. For example: Waterfront, for all bunks, on Tuesday, because of weather. It tells you how many periods it will empty and asks first.

The emptied periods turn yellow on the Build and Schedule tabs, so it is plain what still needs filling. A yellow period goes back to normal as soon as something is put in it. Remove the yellow marks takes the marks away and leaves the periods empty.

Reset

Reset, in red at the top of the Setup tab and in the week bar, is the one way to wipe the slate. It opens a box with three choices and says, in words, what the one you picked will do.

  • Clear everything: every week of the session. Every period of every week is emptied. Your bunks and your settings stay.
  • Clear only Week N. Every period of the week on screen is emptied. The other weeks, your bunks and your settings are not touched.
  • Reset to the base template. The session goes back to exactly how it starts: its own bunks, its own settings and calendar, and no periods filled in. Bunks you added and settings you changed are gone.

In each case the session calendar is put back on the emptied weeks: opening day, the trips and Tiyuls, village day, Mass Program or Color War.

Reset cannot be undone. Download all weeks first if there is anything on the page you might want again.

Build around what you entered

Anything you put in by hand can be kept while the app fills the rest. Enter what you want fixed, press Auto generate, and choose Keep what is there and build around it. Only the empty periods are filled. This is also how you refill yellow periods after a Clear.

Taste of CSL

Bunks named TC1, TC2 and so on are Taste of CSL. They are in camp for week 1 of Session 2 only.

  • They have no league, and nothing of theirs is on the Settings tab: not in the numbers, and not on the calendar table.

  • Auto generate leaves their week exactly as it is.
  • Nobody else is put at a program area in a period Taste of CSL has it. They may all be there together.

5. Auto generate

Auto generate builds the periods for the session's bunks. The button is in step 4 of the Setup tab and in the week bar. It puts the session calendar on first, where it is not there yet, and builds around it and around anything you entered by hand. It reads the other weeks of the session, so each bunk's totals stay fair across the whole session.

What the dialog asks

  • What should be generated? Just Week N, or The whole session, week by week.
  • For one week that already has activities: Keep what is there and build around it (only the empty periods are filled) or Replace what is there (the week is cleared first).
  • For the whole session, a choice for each week:
    • Lock: leave it exactly as it is
    • Build around what is there
    • Regenerate: clear it and build it again
    A week that is already filled in starts as Lock.
  • When replacing, keep Bike Trip and Tiyul where they are. On to start with. Everything on the session calendar is kept the same way.
  • Count what the other weeks already have. On to start with. A bunk that already had Yoga twice in a week you are keeping is not given a third, and its visit numbers carry on from there. Off, the weeks you are not generating are ignored.
  • Leave the days that have already happened as they are (through today). On to start with. Once the session is under way, generating a week again only touches the days still to come, and what already happened still counts toward the totals.

The session is the one picked on the Setup tab, not something the dialog asks.

While it runs

  • It works in the background. You can switch tabs and weeks, look at Tracking, and download.
  • The bar moves with the clock. A week that is done counts in full, and the week being worked on fills in as its time goes by. So the bar keeps moving, jumps ahead when a week comes out early, and is full when the run is over.
  • Beside the time so far it says the most the run can still take, such as "1:19 more at the most". Most runs finish long before that.
  • Each week appears on the page as soon as it is done.
  • The weeks being generated can be looked at but not changed until the run is over.
  • Stop keeps the weeks that are already done and leaves the rest as they were.

How long it takes

A whole session has a time limit: 2 minutes for a full camp of 27 bunks, and less for a smaller camp, down to 75 seconds for 21 bunks or fewer. One week on its own gets a quarter of that (a third in Session 2).

Most runs are far quicker. In our own tests on one computer, Session 1 as it starts (22 bunks) took a few seconds, the full 27-bunk Session 1 took 8, 16 and 19 seconds in three runs, and the full Session 2 took 7 seconds twice. A slower computer will take longer.

The app tries a week several ways at once and keeps the first one that keeps every rule. If none does, it tries again, without saying anything except "try 2" in the panel. A week that comes out early leaves its time to the weeks after it.

When a week will not come out

If a week uses up its time and no version of it keeps every rule, the app hands back the best one it found. The periods that broke a rule are emptied and given another activity where one fits. If every period ends up filled, the week is done like any other.

If some periods are left with nothing that fits, they are empty and marked yellow, and the line after the run says so in place of saying the weeks were generated:

  • Everything that is on the schedule still keeps the rules. Only the yellow periods are missing something.
  • Keep trying puts that week, the week before it and the weeks after it back as they were, and generates them again. The week before goes again because what it used up is the usual reason the next one cannot be finished.
  • Or fill the yellow periods by hand, or change a setting and generate again.

In our tests of this version with the numbers the app starts with, every run of Session 1 came out complete, the full 27-bunk camp included (six runs), and so did every run of the full Session 2 (five runs). The picture above was made by setting numbers that cannot work, on purpose. If the Settings tab says "Unlikely to work" or "Not possible", expect it.

Afterwards

  • Generate again as often as you like. Every run gives a different schedule.
  • Undo puts back every week the run made. It leaves alone anything you changed in other weeks meanwhile.
  • Undo has limits. It only undoes the last run. It goes away as soon as you change a period by hand, upload a file, switch session, or reload the page.

6. The rules it follows

These are the rules a generated week keeps. They are written here in plain words; the app does not list them on screen.

For every bunk

  • Every period has something in it. The one exception is the last Friday of Session 1, which is departure and stays empty.
  • No program area twice in one day.
  • No program area two days in a row. Trips do not count, and Friday into Sunday does not count.
  • Nothing in period 4 and again in period 1 the next morning.
  • Athletics and A&C are single periods, never a double.
  • Athletics and A&C stay within two of each other over the session, and every bunk gets A&C.

For villages

  • Waterfront, league, Shabbat Prep, the swim test, Tiyul and the bike trip are for the whole village at once.
  • Waterfront is a double period, morning or afternoon, one village at a time.
  • Nobody goes to Waterfront before the swim test. In week 1 that means not on the first morning with periods, and not before the village has swum. Mohawk takes its test during General Swim on the first day, so its Waterfront starts on the second.
  • Only so many bunks of one village go to an area in one day. The number is a setting.

Sharing a period

  • Only so many bunks are at an area at once. The number is a setting. Athletics and A&C never take more than three.
  • Bunks only share with bunks they are allowed to be with (chapter 7, Who may share a period).
  • Bunks that share are on the same visit number, so a bunk on its second Teva is not put with a bunk on its first. Athletics and Time with UH are the exceptions to start with.
  • Time with UH and Judaics: two bunks, of the same village.
  • Ropes, Yoga and Ceramics go by campers: bunks of a village go together as long as their campers add up to no more than the limit.

The pool

  • One group at the pool in a period.
  • An Onondaga or Cayuga bunk's first swims are lessons, one bunk alone. How many is a setting.
  • Onondaga and Cayuga never swim together. Tusc always swims as a whole village. Seneca and Mohawk swim together only at the same age.
  • Tusc's triathlon training is never in a period when someone is swimming.

The calendar and Shabbat

  • Nothing on the session calendar is ever moved, and nothing you entered by hand is changed unless you chose to replace the week.
  • A village has Shabbat Prep on Friday afternoon of its Shabbat week, and the extra single periods you set.
  • No bunk has Music or Judaics while any village is at Shabbat Prep, because those specialists run it.
  • Low Ropes comes before High Ropes.

Fairness, and short weeks

  • "Twice a week" means on average over the session. A week the calendar cuts short, such as the week with Mass Program, gets fewer Waterfront and league periods.
  • When the calendar leaves a bunk a good deal less room than an ordinary session, it gets as many visits as fit. It is not treated as a failure.
  • Neighbours in a village are given the same areas each week, so they stay on the same visit and can keep going together.
  • In week 4 of Session 1, which has the least room, two things are looser: A&C partners need not be on the same visit, and a bunk may have one more Time with UH.
  • Ceramics, Judaics and Israel have the fewest places, so they are given their periods first each week. A visit that has no period that week is put off to a later week.
  • Waterfront and league may go one over their number in a week where a half-day is open and the village has room.

Add or remove a program area

When someone is hired for something new, such as archery or martial arts, type its name in the last row of the table, choose its numbers, and press Add. It becomes an activity you can pick on the Build tab, a column on the Tracking tab, a tab in the specialist schedules, and Auto generate gives it to every bunk as single periods. Up to six can be added. An added area has a Remove button beside its name. To stop using an area that came with the app, set it to exactly 0.

Who may share a period

Three choices cover most camps:

  • Inside a village: only the bunk next to it in the list, or any bunk of the village. Starts at any bunk of the village.
  • Paired villages (Onondaga with Cayuga, Seneca with Mohawk): may mix, or never mix. Starts at may mix.
  • Grades: the same grade, within one grade, or any grades. Starts at within one grade.

These are for the areas where bunks may be together. Athletics takes any bunks, Tusc bunks always share with each other, and the pool keeps its own rules whatever is chosen here.

Advanced: customize sharing opens a grid with a box for every pair of bunks. A ticked box means the two may share. Tick or untick any pair; a box you change is outlined, and it stays that way whatever the three choices are set to. Pointing at a box lights up the two bunks it is for. Undo the changes on the grid puts every pair back.

The grid goes by bunk name. A bunk that is renamed starts again from the three choices.

A full camp

The biggest Session 1 camp is 5 Onondaga, 6 Cayuga, 6 Seneca, 6 Mohawk and 4 Tusc: 27 bunks, every cabin. The numbers the app starts with are the ones that many bunks need: 3 bunks of a village a day at every area, and any two bunks of a village within a grade may share. There is nothing to switch on.

If you lower those and a village has six bunks or more, the Settings tab shows a button, Use the suggested settings, and the Auto generate dialog offers the same thing, ticked, to put them back.

A full camp of full bunks is short of room in three places, and the Settings tab says so where it applies. With 15 campers in a bunk, only one bunk fits at Yoga or at Ceramics at a time, so expect some bunks to end the session a visit short at each. And ropes has just enough half-days for everyone twice with no room to spare, so some bunks get one. Smaller bunks ease all three, because two can go together.

Reset to the default settings

Reset to the default settings puts every number back to what the session starts with. It does not touch the schedule. To put the schedule back as well, use Reset.

10. Troubleshooting and questions

It says "Not fully done", and a week has yellow, empty periods.

The app could not find a version of that week that keeps every rule inside its time, so it emptied the periods that broke one and refilled the ones it could. Press Keep trying for another go at that week and the ones around it, fill the yellow periods by hand, or loosen a setting. Look at the top of the Settings tab first: if it says "Unlikely to work" or "Not possible", the numbers are the reason.

The Settings tab says "Will come up short".

That is information, not an error. The calendar leaves less room than the numbers ask for, so some bunks will end the session a visit or two short of something. Auto generate fits in as many as it can. With the numbers each session starts with, neither session says this for its starting bunks; a full Session 1 camp does, for Yoga and ropes.

Not every bunk got ropes twice.

The camp has one ropes course, and one group is there in a half-day. A session only has so many half-days, so there is a limit to how many groups can go, however the schedule is arranged. When the bunks and the ropes settings ask for more than that, the Settings tab says so at the top: "Ropes will not come out for everyone", or "Ropes is not possible for everyone", with the number of half-days and the number of groups. It also says what to try: usually more campers at once at ropes, so more bunks go together.

When ropes does fit, every bunk gets it as often as the setting says. When it does not, everyone gets a first turn before anyone gets a second. Session 1 as it starts fits. Session 2 starts with ropes once, which fits; set it to twice and it does not, because three weeks with Color War in them have about 17 half-days for about 22 groups. The biggest Session 1 camp, 27 bunks at 15 campers each, does not fit at 30 campers at once either; at 45 it does.

The Settings tab says "Not possible" or "Unlikely to work".

The numbers ask for more than there are periods or places. The message says what to try, usually a lower number, or two bunks at once.

It is taking a long time.

A whole session never takes more than 2 minutes, and that long only for a full camp. The panel says the most time that is left. If it is slow every time, close other programs, or other browser tabs with this page open. Stop keeps the weeks that are done.

A bunk ended up in the wrong village.

The village is the first letter of the bunk's name. A bunk called "Onondaga 3" is in Onondaga, and so is one called "Oak". A name that starts with TC is Taste of CSL, not Tusc.

A bunk has no grade or no camper count.

The app still works. Without a grade it goes by the bunk's place in its village, and without a count it assumes 12 campers.

I cannot change a bunk on the Build tab.

Bunks are changed on the Setup tab, in step 2. A change there is made in every week.

I cannot find Download all weeks, Upload or Reset.

Download all weeks and Upload are at the bottom of the Setup tab, under Files. Reset is in red at the top of Setup and in the week bar.

I refreshed the page during a run.

The run stops. The weeks that had already appeared are kept. Press Auto generate again for the rest.

I moved something on the calendar and it did not move on the schedule.

The calendar only fills empty periods. Clear the old one on the Build tab, then press Put the calendar on the schedule now.

Auto generate put a second Tiyul on.

It does not when the one you entered is labelled Tiyul. If you wrote it in under another name, the app cannot tell it is the Tiyul. Use the activity called Tiyul.

The schedule is gone.

The browser's saved data was cleared, or you are on a different browser or computer. Upload your last Excel file.

Something else.

Use Contact support at the bottom of the page.

11. Known limits

  • No login and no cloud. The schedule is in one browser on one computer. Two people cannot work on it at once.
  • Two sessions. Session 1 is four weeks and Session 2 is three. The dates are set for summer 2027 and cannot be changed on screen.
  • Sunday to Friday, four periods a day. Saturday is not on the grid.
  • No phone layout. The grid is wide. Use a laptop or desktop.
  • Day details (R"H and LOD, TS, General Day, DOD, birthdays, EVP, notes) show on the Schedule tab but can only be edited in the Excel file.
  • The app does not know about weather, staffing or one-off events. Enter those by hand, or add a line to the session calendar.
  • A day off for one bunk cannot be put on the calendar. The calendar is for the whole camp or a whole village. Enter it by hand on the Build tab.
  • A bunk is the same in every week. Its name, grades and campers cannot differ from one week to the next. The one exception is Taste of CSL, which is in week 1 of Session 2 only.
  • The how-often menu is on six rows, not every one: Time with UH, Judaics, Israel, Teva, Yoga and added areas. The other areas are set by the week, exactly, or village by village.
  • In Session 2, Tusc's ropes is High Ropes. That comes with the session and is not on the Settings tab.
  • A hand-entered period is trusted. When building around what you entered, the app does not check your own entries against the sharing rules.
  • Undo only covers the last Auto generate, and only until you change something.
  • 27 bunks is the most. The camp has 27 cabins.
  • A complete schedule cannot be promised for every setting. The Settings tab lets you ask for more than fits. What is promised is that a run ends inside its time, that everything it puts on the schedule keeps the rules, and that it says "Not fully done" when periods are left empty.
